# SmoothGo — Q&A Bank

Use short answers. Do not read from the slides. If you do not know, say what you would test next.

## 1. How accurate is your crowd data?

In the prototype, the data is illustrative. In the pilot, we would combine three sources: venue-reported signals, opt-in user reports and visible queue estimates. We would show a confidence level rather than pretending every number is exact.

## 2. What if a venue bans paid queue assistance?

We do not enter venues that ban it. The first pilot focuses on restaurants and non-strict queue environments. In scenic areas, SmoothGo is information-only until the venue confirms the rules.

## 3. Why would a traveller pay for this?

Because the value is measured in time. A short queue-assist fee can protect a meal, a train or the last hour of a trip. The base planner can stay free; users pay only when the convenience is worth it.

## 4. How are you different from Trip.com or Meituan?

They are strong at discovery, booking and reviews. SmoothGo is a decision layer: it connects preferences, live crowd signals and a cross-venue plan, then replans when conditions change.

## 5. Why start in Changchun?

It is a realistic pilot market for us: local knowledge, lower acquisition cost, a compact set of attractions and access to university talent. We can prove the operating model before expanding.

## 6. What is the biggest risk?

Trust. If the crowd estimate is wrong or a queue assistant behaves badly, users lose confidence. That is why we start with transparent estimates, verified assistants, ratings and clear handoff rules.

## 7. How do you make money?

Three streams: a commission on completed queue-assist requests, merchant promotion for partner venues, and a premium subscription for advanced forecasting and multi-day planning. We do not sell user data.

## 8. What stops a large platform from copying you?

They can copy the feature, but local execution still matters: venue relationships, verified assistants, user-reported data and a trusted local brand. We would also be a potential partner rather than only a competitor.

## 9. What is your next milestone?

An eight-week Changchun pilot with ten venues and one thousand users. We would measure completed plans, replan rate, queue minutes saved and repeat use.

## 10. Is this fair to people who cannot pay?

The planning and crowd information should remain free. Queue assistance is optional and limited. We do not sell priority entry or tickets, and we avoid venues where the practice is prohibited.
