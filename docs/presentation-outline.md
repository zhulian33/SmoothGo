# SmoothGo — English Presentation Outline

**SmoothGo: Give travellers their time back**

AI-assisted travel planning for Changchun

Presented by: Member A · Member B · Member C · Member D · Member E · Member F

**Speaker notes:** Open with a direct question: “When you plan a trip, how much time do you spend deciding where to go next?” Pause. Then say: “SmoothGo is built for the part of travel that most apps ignore: the moment your plan stops working.”

---

## Slide 2 — The problem

**The itinerary is not the hard part. The queue is.**

- Travellers lose hours waiting, not exploring.
- Plans break when crowds, weather or opening hours change.
- Most apps tell you what exists; few tell you what is worth doing **now**.

**Speaker notes:** Explain the gap between discovery and decision. A traveller does not need another list of 50 attractions; they need a clear answer to “What should I do next, given the current crowd?”

---

## Slide 3 — Our solution

**A day plan that changes when the city does**

SmoothGo combines:

1. Personal preferences
2. Live crowd and queue signals
3. A continuously updated route

Optional: verified queue assistance where venue rules allow.

**Speaker notes:** Keep this simple. SmoothGo is not a booking site. It is a decision layer that helps a traveller choose the next stop and adjust the day.

---

## Slide 4 — Target customers

**Three travellers, one shared pain**

- **Independent travellers:** want flexibility without wasting time.
- **Families:** need a realistic pace and fewer meltdowns.
- **Business travellers:** have short windows and high time value.

Initial market: Changchun, Jilin Province.

**Speaker notes:** Explain why Changchun is a sensible pilot: local knowledge, lower acquisition cost, a compact set of attractions and a university talent pool. The product can later expand to Xi’an, Chongqing and Changsha.

---

## Slide 5 — Product walkthrough

**From three preferences to a calmer day**

1. Choose interests, pace and start time.
2. Receive a time-boxed itinerary.
3. See live crowd and queue signals.
4. Tap “Replan” when a stop gets busy.
5. Request optional queue assistance.

**Speaker notes:** Run the demo or play the 60–90 second recording. Point out the exact moment the plan changes. Do not narrate every screen; focus on the value of the replan.

---

## Slide 6 — How the recommendation works

**A transparent score, not a black box**

The prototype scores each place on:

- interest match;
- current crowd pressure;
- expected queue time;
- travel time;
- rating and time fit.

Production version: live venue APIs + user reports + learned crowd forecasting.

**Speaker notes:** Be honest: the prototype uses illustrative data and a rule-based scorer. This is a strength for a student project because the logic is explainable. The production roadmap is where the real data partnerships matter.

---

## Slide 7 — Market and competition

**Discovery is crowded. Decision support is not.**

| Platform type | What it does well | What it leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| Trip.com / Ctrip | Booking and discovery | Little dynamic replanning around queues |
| Meituan / Dianping | Local food and reviews | Fragmented across venues; not a full-day plan |
| Travel content apps | Inspiration | No live operational decision |
| Queue tools | Single-venue waiting | No personalised itinerary |

**Speaker notes:** Do not attack competitors. Position SmoothGo as a layer that can eventually partner with them. The gap is the live, cross-venue decision.

---

## Slide 8 — Business model

**We earn when we save time, not when we add clutter**

- **Queue-assist commission:** 15–20% of each completed request.
- **Merchant promotion:** priority placement for partner venues.
- **Premium subscription:** advanced forecasting and multi-day planning.
- **Launch offers:** first queue assist free; off-peak discounts.

**Speaker notes:** Emphasise that the base planner can be free. Revenue comes from optional convenience and merchant tools, not from selling user data.

---

## Slide 9 — Operations and premises

**Light asset, local first**

- **Premises:** university incubator or co-working space; no physical stores.
- **Data:** venue partnerships, public tourism data and opt-in user reports.
- **Supply:** vetted part-time queue assistants.
- **Handoff:** QR confirmation at the venue.
- **Compliance:** information-only in scenic areas until rules are confirmed.

**Speaker notes:** This slide answers the “premises” requirement directly. The business is software-led and does not need to build or buy tourism infrastructure.

---

## Slide 10 — Marketing and roadmap

**Start where the queue is visible**

- Short videos: “How long is the queue at ___ right now?”
- QR codes at partner venues and campus touchpoints.
- Travel-agency add-on for independent travellers.
- Roadmap: Changchun → Xi’an → Chongqing → Changsha.

**Speaker notes:** The first campaign should be local and measurable: one city, ten venues, one thousand users. Avoid claiming national reach before the pilot.

---

## Slide 11 — Funding and P&L

**A lean pilot with a clear path to break-even**

Illustrative first-year assumptions:

- Start-up budget: ¥80,000.
- Funding: team contribution + university innovation fund + small local angel.
- Main costs: development, cloud/map APIs, marketing and assistant operations.
- Revenue target: 1,000 completed queue-assist requests in the pilot.
- Break-even target: month 10 after launch.

**Speaker notes:** Label every number as an assumption. The point is to show financial logic, not to pretend the pilot has real traction. Check the math before presenting.

---

## Slide 12 — Challenges

**The hard part is trust, not code**

| Challenge | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Crowd data is inaccurate at first | Start with user reports and visible queue estimates; partner with venues |
| Paid queueing may be seen as unfair | Prioritise restaurants and non-strict queues; information-only in scenic areas |
| Assistants may fail or behave poorly | ID verification, ratings, deposits and clear handoff rules |
| Demand is seasonal | Add local-life use cases and off-peak promotions |

**Speaker notes:** Discuss the compliance issue openly. A foreign teacher will appreciate that the team has thought about fairness, not just growth.

---

## Slide 13 — Why SmoothGo matters

**A small shift in timing can change a whole day**

- Clear pain: waiting wastes a traveller’s most limited resource.
- Simple promise: see more, stand in line less.
- Light model: software, partnerships and local assistants.
- Scalable path: one city, then a repeatable playbook.

**Pilot ask:** 10 Changchun venues, 1,000 users, one 8-week trial.

**Speaker notes:** End with the takeaway, not a generic “thank you.” Then invite questions.
